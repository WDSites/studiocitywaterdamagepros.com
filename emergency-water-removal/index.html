What emergency water removal covers

Emergency water removal, also called water extraction, is the job of getting liquid water out of the building as fast as possible. In Studio City that usually means a burst pipe, an overflowing appliance, a failed water heater or water that came in from outside and now sits on the floor.

Speed matters because water does not stay where it lands. It wicks up drywall, runs under baseboards and soaks into carpet pad within minutes. Every inch it travels is more material that has to be dried or replaced.

This page is about the first step only. Once the standing water is out, water mitigation takes over with drying equipment and moisture readings. If the loss came from storm runoff or groundwater, flood damage restoration covers the added cleanup and safety steps.

When standing water is an emergency

Call right away if water is deeper than the soles of your shoes, is still coming in, touches outlets or appliances, or could be contaminated. Until a crew arrives, these steps protect people and limit the damage.

Stop the source

Close the main water valve or the fixture shutoff. If you cannot find it, call the line and the dispatcher will talk you through it.

Power safety

Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. If water is near the panel, outlets or gas appliances, stay out and call 911 or the utility first.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, shoes, electronics, paper and small furniture off wet floors. Place foil or blocks under furniture legs.

Mind the water

Keep children and pets out. Treat water from a toilet, drain or outside as contaminated and do not wade through it.

Burst pipe water damage: the first hour

A burst or failed pipe is the most common reason people call. Under a sink, behind a washing machine, inside a wall, at the ice maker line or at the water heater, the pattern is the same. The first job is the shutoff, the second is the extraction, and the third is finding out how far the water went.

Know which side of the meter the problem is on. A break in the street or at the meter is the utility's job, so report it to LADWP or call 311. Water inside the house or in a line on your side is yours to stop and clean up.

In 2014 an 8-inch water main broke on Halkirk Street, south of Ventura Boulevard, and LADWP said about 56,000 gallons escaped. Water reached yards and some nearby homes on Halkirk and Goodland Avenue. After any street main break, check garages, crawl spaces and low rooms for water that came in quietly, even if the house looks dry from the street.

What happens after the water is out

Pumping is not drying. A floor can look nearly dry while the pad, subfloor and wall bottoms still hold water. That is why the crew keeps going with air movers, dehumidifiers and daily moisture readings until the numbers match the dry standard.

If materials had to be removed or the water was dirty, water damage cleanup handles the clearing and sanitizing. The crew documents each stage with photos and readings, which also helps if you file an insurance claim.

What affects the price of water removal

Scope and price are explained before work starts. The main factors are:

  • Depth and volume. More water takes longer to extract and often needs a pump first.
  • Water category. Dirty water adds protective steps and disposal.
  • Floor and wall materials. Carpet, pad, tile and hardwood each need a different method.
  • Access. Crawl spaces, upper floors and tight rooms take more setup time.
  • Drying that follows. Extraction is usually the start of a multi-day drying job.

Water removal questions in Studio City

Do you handle water removal emergencies at night or on weekends in Studio City?

Yes. Dispatch is open 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Crews are sent same day when available. We do not promise an exact arrival time, but the dispatcher gives you safe steps for the minutes before the crew arrives.

Should I use a shop vacuum on standing water?

A household shop vacuum can help with a small puddle on a hard floor if no electrical risk exists. It cannot handle inches of water, and it will not reach water in carpet pad or wall cavities.

How do I know if the water is contaminated?

Water from a toilet, a drain, a sewer backup or the outdoors should be treated as contaminated. Clean supply-line water can degrade if it sits. When in doubt, stay out and call.

What if the pipe that broke is in the street?

A break in the street or at the meter belongs to the utility. Call LADWP or 311. If that water enters your property, call the restoration line for the cleanup.

Will you shut off my water for me?

The dispatcher can talk you through finding and closing your main valve. If the failed line is inside a wall, the crew isolates it and confirms the water has stopped before extraction.

Studio City and the towns around it

Studio City is in the southeast San Fernando Valley, just west of the Cahuenga Pass. Our base at 13223 Ventura Blvd puts crews on the main east-west road of the Valley. From there dispatch reaches the neighborhoods and cities listed below.

Each page below is written for that place, with its own housing history, landmarks and ZIP codes. The distances are rough straight-line estimates, not drive-time promises.

[Sherman Oaks](/service-area/sherman-oaks/)

About 2 miles west. ZIP 91423 and 91403. Founded in 1927, with the Los Angeles River on its northern edge.

[North Hollywood](/service-area/north-hollywood/)

About 3 miles northeast. ZIP 91605, 91601, 91606 and 91602. The NoHo Arts District and a long renter-heavy housing mix.

[Burbank](/service-area/burbank/)

About 5 miles east. ZIP 91505, 91506, 91504, 91501 and 91502. A separate city with older housing and its own permit office.

[Valley Village](/service-area/valley-village/)

About 3 miles north. ZIP 91607. Studio-era houses and thousands of apartment units.

[Encino](/service-area/encino/)

About 6 miles west. ZIP 91436 and 91316. Larger lots on the Santa Monica Mountains slope.

How to read the distances

The mile counts above are straight-line estimates from the Studio City base. Real driving routes follow Ventura Boulevard, the Ventura Freeway and the Hollywood Freeway, and traffic changes them from hour to hour. Treat the numbers as a sense of scale and not as a time estimate.

The dispatcher gives the most honest picture when you call, because the answer depends on the hour, the weather and where crews are working at that moment.

Why local detail matters

A water loss in a 1950s Valley ranch house is not the same job as one in a newer condo or a hillside home. Pipe age, slab or raised floors, attic access and nearby slopes all change how water moves and how long materials stay wet.

That is why each town page describes the housing it actually has, the landmarks crews use for directions and the ZIP codes dispatch checks first. For flood zones, check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your city building department about local rules.

Crews and coverage on busy days

After a big storm or a main break, many calls come in at once. Dispatch works through them by danger first, so active flooding and sewage come before a slow stain. Tell the dispatcher honestly how fast the water is moving and it will be sorted correctly.

Before you call

Have the street address, the nearest cross street and a rough idea of where the water started. A quick description of what is wet, such as one room, a whole floor or a garage, helps the dispatcher decide what to send. You do not need ZIP code or town name memorized, because the dispatcher will confirm both.

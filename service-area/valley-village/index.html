Cooling season and rainy season in Valley Village

Water problems here follow the calendar. In the long cooling season, air conditioner condensate lines clog and drip into closets and ceilings. In the rainy months, roof leaks and runoff find the weak spots. Plumbing leaks can happen in any month.

A short seasonal check helps: clear the AC drain line and pan before summer, look at the roof and gutters before winter, and know where the shutoff is all year.

When water is spreading in Valley Village

Call the 24/7 line as soon as you see it. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, and the dispatcher explains the first safe steps. Crews reach most addresses by Laurel Canyon Boulevard or Riverside Drive.

Shut off the supply

Close the unit shutoff or main valve. In a building with shared lines, call the manager at the same time.

Unplug and stay clear

Turn off power to wet rooms only if safe. Water near outlets means call 911 or the utility first.

Catch and lift

Put a bucket under ceiling drips, lift belongings and mop up only clean water.

Tell the neighbors

A leak that drops through a ceiling hits the unit below. Let them know.

A neighborhood built on apartments and studio-era houses

Many Valley Village houses were built in the 1930s by studio workers. A 2006 description called them mostly single-story Spanish and ranch-style homes of about 1,700 square feet. The same description counted about 3,881 single-family homes, 1,073 condos and 8,213 apartment units. In the 2000 census about 68.7 percent of housing was renter-occupied.

With that many apartments, a leak is often a neighbor's. If water comes through your ceiling from another unit, photograph it, tell your landlord or HOA, and ask your insurer what applies. We provide readings and photos you can share.

Landmarks and boundaries

Valley Village is bounded by Burbank Boulevard on the north, the Tujunga Wash on the west, the Ventura Freeway on the south and the Hollywood Freeway on the east, according to the neighborhood council. Valley Village Park on Westpark Drive and the Tujunga Wash Greenway are handy reference points when you give directions.

The neighborhood marked its borders with seven blue reflective markers in 1991. If your address sits right on a boundary, give the dispatcher your street name and cross street.

Water is coming through my ceiling from the unit above. What do I do?

Photograph it, tell your landlord or HOA, contain what you can and call the line. Who pays depends on your lease or HOA rules, so ask your insurer.

How do I protect my AC from causing water damage?

Ask your HVAC technician about a drain line flush, a drip pan and a float switch before the cooling season starts.
